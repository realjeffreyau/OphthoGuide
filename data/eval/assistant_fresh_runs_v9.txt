[4/8] f04 Q: What are the side effects of LASIK?

The side effects of LASIK include dry eye, seeing a glare or halo around bright lights, double vision, blurry vision, and sensitivity to light [nei_049_chunk_02]. These side effects usually go away after a few months [nei_049_chunk_02]. Rarely, these problems can be permanent [nei_049_chunk_02]. Other risks of LASIK include infection, problems with corneal healing, vision loss, or seeing less clearly than before surgery [nei_049_chunk_02]. If LASIK causes vision loss, you may need another surgery to fix it [nei_049_chunk_02]. Rarely, vision loss from LASIK may be permanent [nei_049_chunk_02]. [6/8] f06 Q: How do doctors test for dry eye?

Doctors test for dry eye using several methods [nei_040_chunk_01] ◐. A slit lamp test involves using a microscope to examine the eyes after applying a dye that makes tears easier to see, allowing the doctor to assess tear production and quality [nei_040_chunk_01]. A Schirmer’s test measures tear production by placing a piece of paper on the eyelid and observing moisture after five minutes [nei_040_chunk_01]. A tear break up time (TBUT) test checks how long the tear film lasts after blinking by applying dye and observing the duration the dyed film remains on the eye [nei_040_chunk_02] ◐. Additionally, a comprehensive dilated eye exam may include checking tear amount, tear drying time, and eyelid structure [nei_006_chunk_03]. These tests help determine if the eyes are producing enough tears or if tears are drying too quickly, which are key indicators of dry eye [nei_040_chunk_01] ◐. This is general education, not a diagnosis.
