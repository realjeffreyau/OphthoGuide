[6/10] a06 Q: What are the shots in the eye for wet macular degeneration?

The shots in the eye for wet macular degeneration are anti-VEGF injections [nei_032_chunk_01] ◐. These medicines help stop bleeding and leaking from blood vessels in the back of the eye [nei_032_chunk_01]. Anti-VEGF injections are the most common treatment doctors use to slow vision loss from wet AMD [nei_032_chunk_01]. They involve putting numbing medicine in the eye, cleaning the eye to prevent infection, and injecting the anti-VEGF medicine with a very small needle [nei_032_chunk_01]. Anti-VEGF injections usually only work for a short time, so most people need to get them many times [nei_032_chunk_02]. At first, most people need injections once a month, and over time, the frequency may decrease [nei_037_chunk_02]. Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a much less common treatment for wet AMD that doctors sometimes use along with anti-VEGF injections [nei_032_chunk_02]. PDT uses a light-sensitive medicine called verteporfin that turns on when hit by a specific type of laser, causing the verteporfin to break down the blood vessels [nei_032_chunk_02] ◐. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

[7/10] a07 Q: What is the difference between nearsighted and farsighted?

Nearsightedness occurs when light focuses in front of the retina, making far-away objects blurry [nei_010_chunk_02]. Farsightedness occurs when light focuses behind the retina, making nearby objects blurry [nei_011_chunk_02]. Nearsightedness usually starts between ages 6 and 14 and gets worse until your early twenties [nei_010_chunk_02]. Most people who are farsighted are born with it, but it may not cause vision problems until you get older [nei_011_chunk_02]. The most common treatments for nearsightedness are eyeglasses or contact lenses, with surgery available for adults [nei_010_chunk_02]. The most common treatments for farsightedness are eyeglasses or contact lenses, with surgery available for adults [nei_011_chunk_02]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.
