AREDS 2 supplements contain specific vitamins and minerals that are recommended for macular degeneration [nei_031_chunk_03]. These include Vitamin C (500 mg), Vitamin E (400 IU), Copper (2 mg), Zinc (80 mg), Lutein (10 mg), and Zeaxanthin (2 mg) [nei_031_chunk_03]. Beta-carotene is not included in AREDS 2, which is a difference from the older AREDS formulation [nei_031_chunk_02]. f02 Q: How long does pink eye last?

Viral pink eye, the most common type, usually gets better in 1 to 2 weeks without medicine [nei_046_chunk_01]. Sometimes, it can take 3 weeks or more to heal [nei_046_chunk_01]. Bacterial pink eye usually gets better in 2 to 5 days, but it can take 2 weeks or more to go away completely [nei_046_chunk_02] ◐. The type of pink eye you have will affect how long it takes to heal [nei_046_chunk_01]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

f03 Q: What is recovery like after cataract surgery?

After cataract surgery, you’ll need to rest in a recovery area outside the operating room for a little while [nei_033_chunk_04]. Before going home, the medical team will check to make sure you don’t have any problems with your eye [nei_033_chunk_04]. Your eye doctor will explain how to protect your eye after surgery [nei_033_chunk_04]. They’ll give you eye drops to help your eye heal, and you may need to wear a special eye shield or glasses [nei_033_chunk_04]. Your eye may feel a bit itchy or uncomfortable and sensitive to light and touch [nei_033_chunk_04]. After 1 or 2 days, your eye should feel better [nei_033_chunk_04]. Most people are completely healed 8 weeks after their surgery [nei_033_chunk_05]. Your eye doctor will schedule checkups to make sure your eye is healing correctly [nei_033_chunk_04]. Cataract surgery is one of the most common, safe, and effective types of surgery done in the United States [nei_001_chunk_02]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

f04 Q: What are the side effects of LASIK?

The side effects of LASIK include dry eye, seeing a glare or halo around bright lights, double vision, blurriness, and sensitivity to light [nei_049_chunk_02]. These side effects usually go away after a few months [nei_049_chunk_02]. Rarely, these problems can be permanent [nei_049_chunk_02]. Other risks of LASIK include infection, problems with corneal healing, or vision loss, which may require further surgery [nei_049_chunk_02]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

f06 Q: How do doctors test for dry eye?

Doctors test for dry eye using several methods, including the slit lamp test and Schirmer’s test [nei_040_chunk_01]. The slit lamp test involves using a microscope to examine tear production after applying a dye [nei_040_chunk_01]. The Schirmer’s test uses numbing drops and a piece of paper to measure tear moisture over five minutes [nei_040_chunk_01]. A tear break up time (TBUT) test checks how long the tear film lasts after blinking by applying dye and observing the duration the dyed film remains on the eye [nei_040_chunk_02] ◐. These tests help determine if the eyes are producing enough tears or if tears are drying too quickly, which are key indicators of dry eye [nei_040_chunk_01] ◐. The doctor may also check the structure of the eyelids during a comprehensive dilated eye exam [nei_006_chunk_03]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

f07 Q: What laser treatment is used for diabetic retinopathy?

For diabetic retinopathy, a type of laser treatment called scatter laser surgery (also known as panretinal photocoagulation) is used [nei_038_chunk_01] ◐. This procedure involves using a medical laser to shrink blood vessels in the eye that are causing vision problems [nei_038_chunk_01] ◐. The treatment is performed in the eye doctor’s office, and your eye will be numbed before the procedure [nei_038_chunk_01] ◐. During the treatment, you may see flashes of light and your eye may sting or feel uncomfortable [nei_038_chunk_01]. Your vision will be blurry for the rest of the day, so you’ll need someone to drive you home [nei_038_chunk_01]. You may need more than 1 session of scatter laser surgery [nei_038_chunk_01]. This treatment has risks, including possible loss of peripheral (side) vision, color vision, and night vision, but for many people, the benefits of this treatment outweigh the risks [nei_038_chunk_01]. This is general education, not a diagnosis.

f08 Q: Is glaucoma always caused by high eye pressure?

No, glaucoma is not always caused by high eye pressure [nei_044_chunk_04] ◐. The evidence shows that while high eye pressure increases the risk of glaucoma, it is not the only cause [nei_041_chunk_01] ◐. For example, some people with normal eye pressure can still develop glaucoma, as mentioned in [nei_041_chunk_01]. Additionally, there are types of glaucoma, such as pigmentary glaucoma, which occurs when pigment from the iris blocks fluid drainage, and neovascular glaucoma, which is caused by abnormal blood vessel growth due to conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure [nei_044_chunk_04] ◐. This is general education, not a diagnosis.
