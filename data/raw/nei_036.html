Cataracts and the Eye

A cataract is a cloudy area in the lens of your eye that can cause vision problems. Click or tap the image below to explore how cataracts affect the eye. Look at the different parts of the eye and see the difference between mild, moderate, and severe cataracts.

Optic Nerve

The optic nerve sends signals from the retina to the brain, which turns them into the images you see. Because cataracts make it harder for the eye to focus light on the retina, the optic nerve can't send clear signals to the brain. That's why your vision is blurry, hazy, or less colorful when you have cataracts.

Retina

The retina is the light-sensitive layer of tissue at the back of the eye. Cataracts can make it harder for the lens of your eye to focus light on the retina.

Sclera

The sclera is the layer of white tissue that covers most of the outside of the eye. It helps your eye keep its shape and protects it from injury.

Cornea

The cornea is the clear front layer of the eye. The cornea works with the lens (where cataracts happen) to help focus light on the retina.

Iris

The iris is the colored part of the eye that controls how much light the pupil lets in.

Pupil

The pupil is an opening in the eye just inside the cornea. After light passes through the cornea, some of the light enters the pupil and goes through the lens.

Lens

The lens is the clear inner part of the eye that helps focus light on the retina. A cataract is a cloudy area in the lens of your eye.
